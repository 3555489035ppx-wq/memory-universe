# Memory Universe · Case Study

## 1. Project Background

People collect more photos than ever, but photo libraries make it difficult to revisit relationships, mood, and narrative. Memory Universe explores how a personal archive can become an experience.

## 2. User Problem

Linear timelines flatten memories into thumbnails. Manual video editing is costly, and cloud-based organization introduces privacy and availability concerns.

## 3. Product Goal

Let users explore and replay a meaningful period through time, people, place, emotion, music, and spatial relationships while keeping personal data local by default.

## 4. My Responsibilities

Product framing, emotional interaction model, 3D information architecture, AI boundary design, template flow, local-first storage strategy, frontend implementation, demo content, and project documentation.

## 5. Product Flow

Choose theme → Add memories → Choose music → Organize → Generate a 3D universe → Explore → Dive in / create constellation / replay / export.

## 6. AI Workflow

Input: photos, EXIF, user notes, music preference, and selected theme.

Context: time, people, place, emotion, relationships, and template constraints.

Processing: cluster memories, summarize, suggest relationships, and propose narrative pacing.

Output: tags, story blocks, template suggestions, confidence, and reviewable metadata.

Human review: confirm, edit, reject, or regenerate before anything is persisted as organization metadata.

## 7. Design Decisions

3D is used for relationships; music is part of the timeline; local-first storage is the starting trust decision; future AI remains separate from originals and requires human confirmation.

## 8. Demo Result

The [Live Demo](https://memory-universe-two.vercel.app/) opens the product homepage. Click **体验高中回忆 Demo** to enter the complete high-school memory template with built-in images and local music; the core experience takes about three minutes.

## 9. Future Plan

Auditable AI organization, additional templates, encrypted cross-device backup, sharing controls, reduced-motion improvements, and low-end device performance validation are the next steps.
